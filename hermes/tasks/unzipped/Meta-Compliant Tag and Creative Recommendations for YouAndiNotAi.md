# Meta-Compliant Tag and Creative Recommendations for YouAndiNotAi

Prepared by **Manus AI**

## Executive Summary

**YouAndiNotAi** is positioned most safely on Meta platforms as a **human-first social platform for real-world connection**, with emphasis on **verification, trust, and authenticity** rather than direct promises about dating success. This is important because Meta treats dating ads as a sensitive category that generally requires prior written permission, limits targeting to adults, and prohibits sexualized positioning, deceptive claims, and ad copy that asserts or implies sensitive personal attributes.[1] [2]

The most defensible strategic direction is therefore to present the platform as a **verified, human-centered alternative to low-trust online social experiences**. The creative should feel modern and emotionally resonant, but it should avoid making the viewer feel diagnosed, exposed, or romantically deficient. That means the safest messaging focuses on the product itself: real people, identity-first trust, and AI used as a protective layer rather than a social substitute.

## Policy Framing That Matters Most

The policy backdrop is straightforward. Meta states that dating ads require prior written permission and must be targeted to people **18 years or older**.[1] Meta also disallows ads for dating services that emphasize sexual encounters, casual sexual themes, certain financial dating arrangements, affairs or infidelity, or fictitious individuals or groups.[1] In addition, Meta's advertising standards prohibit adult nudity, sexually suggestive content, deceptive practices, and ad content that implies or exploits negative self-perception in sensitive contexts.[2]

| Policy area | What it means for YouAndiNotAi | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dating ad category | The platform appears to qualify as a dating-related service | Seek category approval before launch on Meta [1] |
| Age restrictions | Ads must only target adults | Set age floor to 18+ [1] |
| Sexual content restrictions | No suggestive poses, bedroom framing, or erotic styling | Use clean, social, lifestyle visuals [2] |
| Personal attributes restrictions | Do not imply the viewer is lonely, rejected, single, or unsafe | Avoid “Are you tired of fake profiles?” style hooks [2] |
| Misleading practices restrictions | No guaranteed outcomes or exaggerated trust claims | Avoid “find your person fast” or “100% real people” claims unless provable [2] |

## Recommended Positioning

The strongest compliant positioning for Meta is not “the dating app that fixes your love life,” but rather **“a human-first platform built to reduce bot noise and make real connection feel more trustworthy.”** This approach matches the public site language and aligns better with policy constraints. It emphasizes platform architecture and product intent instead of making emotional diagnoses about the audience.

A second important positioning choice is to keep **AI** in a supporting role. The site already does this well by explaining that AI protects the experience rather than performing connection. That distinction should remain central in ads. It helps the brand feel differentiated without creating the impression that the platform is deceptive, automated, or emotionally manipulative.

## High-Risk Tags to Avoid

Some language may sound catchy but is more likely to create rejection risk or poor reviewer interpretation. The main issue is not only explicit policy violation, but also whether the language sounds too personal, too sexual, or too absolute.

| Avoid tag or angle | Why it is risky |
|---|---|
| **No More Fake People** | Can imply personal harm or negative audience condition |
| **Tired of Fake Profiles?** | Directly addresses the viewer’s presumed experience |
| **Stop Getting Ignored** | Implies a sensitive personal attribute or dating failure |
| **Find Better Matches Fast** | Performance or outcome promise |
| **Finally Meet Your Type** | Implied outcome and romantic certainty |
| **The Anti-Tinder** | Comparative framing can be usable, but often distracts from policy-safe value claims |
| **Guaranteed Real Singles** | Absolute claim may be hard to substantiate |
| **Hotter, Safer Dating** | Suggestive and potentially low-trust framing |

## Image Direction

The strongest images for Meta should feel **editorial, modern, and human**, not seductive, hyper-polished, or obviously “dating-ad” coded. The safest creative strategy is to show **realistic adult social energy** without overt romance, sexual tension, or exclusionary beauty cues. This reduces policy risk while giving the brand a premium feel.

| Concept | Visual description | Why it is suitable |
|---|---|---|
| Urban coffee meetup | Two adults in a bright café having an easy, natural conversation across a small table | Signals real-world connection without sexualization |
| Side-by-side walk | Two adults walking outdoors in a city neighborhood, relaxed and mid-conversation | Communicates authenticity and momentum |
| Group social setting | Three or four adults at a casual rooftop or community setting, one pair speaking naturally in foreground | Broadens brand beyond pure dating language |
| Verification lifestyle visual | A close-up of a hand holding a phone with abstract verification UI, with a warm human scene in soft background | Anchors the trust story without needing screenshots |
| Quiet confidence portrait | A single adult in natural light looking calm and self-assured, with understated styling | Good for trust-centered campaigns, but should remain inclusive and non-model-like |

## Image Directions to Avoid

Images can trigger problems even when copy is clean. For this brand, risk comes mainly from suggestive styling, exaggerated body emphasis, and imagery that looks like hookup advertising rather than a human-first platform.

| Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|
| Bedroom or couch intimacy scenes | Too easily read as sexual or suggestive |
| Cleavage, shirtless, or body-emphasis framing | Unnecessary policy risk |
| “Hot singles” nightclub look | Low-trust, sexually charged impression |
| Screens full of romantic promises or match counts | Can look deceptive or outcome-driven |
| Heavy red-heart visual overload | Makes the brand feel like a cliché dating ad and can invite wrong categorization |

## Best Creative Route

If the goal is to perform well while staying safe, I recommend a **trust-led lifestyle route**. The palette should borrow from the site’s strong black-white-orange brand feel, but the imagery itself should be softer and more natural than the landing page typography. That contrast will help the ads feel premium and social rather than aggressive.

The best first-wave creative set should therefore combine **one trust-forward concept**, **one social-lifestyle concept**, and **one feature-trust concept**. This gives enough variation to test without drifting into risky territory.

## Operational Recommendations

Before using these creatives in live ads, the account setup should reflect the fact that this appears to be a dating-related service under Meta’s rules. The launch should therefore include category review readiness, adult-only targeting, and a landing-page consistency check so that the ad promise, site language, and verification claims all align. The cleaner and more feature-led that alignment is, the better the approval odds are likely to be.[1] [2]

The workbook you shared is useful mainly as a **distribution planning resource**. Its best contribution to the Meta strategy is not the more promotional prompt language, but rather its emphasis on visual storytelling, short-form hooks, and community-adjacent conversation themes. For Meta specifically, those ideas should be translated into a calmer and more policy-aware form.
