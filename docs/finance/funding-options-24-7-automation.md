# Funding options for a 24/7 fully automated mission

Purpose: identify practical funding mechanisms that can sustain always-on automation, monitoring, routing, lead generation, safety tooling, and mission-control infrastructure without depending on a single fragile revenue source.

Context used: the current 3-year P&L/runway summary shows base-case self-sustainability in M09, conservative self-sustainability in M19, and the main sensitivity drivers are conversion rate, active-user growth, marketing spend, and contractor/dev cost. This funding plan therefore favors channels that either validate recurring demand quickly or offset infrastructure costs directly.

Doctrine guardrails:

- ANTIGRAVITY / YouAndINotAI remains a for-profit LLC, not a nonprofit.
- Keep the 10% #ForTheKids / contractual mission reserve as the cap and default model.
- Customer-facing copy should avoid restricted public-benefit or fundraising-style framing; describe commercial products, memberships, services, partnership fees, sponsorship inventory, grants, or contractual revenue disbursement as appropriate.
- This document is strategy research only, not legal, tax, securities, grant-compliance, or incorporation advice.

## Executive recommendation

Prototype these first:

1. B2B automation retainers and support contracts: fastest path to cash, low technical novelty, and aligned with existing lead-gen/service work.
2. Membership / SaaS tiers for YouAndINotAI: recurring revenue that compounds with the community and directly supports the 24/7 platform.
3. Sponsorship and partnership packages: monetizes community programming and mission-control visibility without waiting for large user scale.

Keep grant programs, open-source sponsorships, API/data products, and compute resale as parallel second-wave tracks. They can be valuable, but each requires either proof of traction, compliance overhead, or stronger technical packaging before becoming dependable.

## Funding mechanisms

### 1. Membership / SaaS subscription tiers

Offer paid recurring tiers for YouAndINotAI members, teams, organizers, or partner communities. Tiers can package verification, safer matching, community spaces, event tools, moderation, higher support priority, advanced matching quality, or analytics. This fits 24/7 automation because revenue scales with active usage while the platform's automated systems perform the matching, moderation, routing, notifications, and reliability work continuously.

- Initial setup cost: Medium. Requires pricing pages, Square subscription wiring, feature gates, account/billing support flows, and clear compliant copy.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Needs churn tracking, support, payment failure recovery, feature delivery, and community management.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires enough users or organizations who see recurring value; strongest once the platform has verified users and recurring partner events.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: High. Recurring revenue maps directly to always-on service availability and reliability commitments.
- Scalability: High if acquisition and retention work; pricing can evolve from individual to group/community tiers.

### 2. B2B automation retainers and managed service contracts

Sell monthly automation packages to small businesses, creators, local organizations, or platform operators: lead capture, inbox triage, customer-service assistants, workflow automation, dashboards, analytics, and follow-up systems. This is not a one-off build model; it is an ongoing support-and-operations retainer where the automated system runs 24/7 and ANTIGRAVITY monitors, improves, and reports on it.

- Initial setup cost: Low to Medium. Requires repeatable service packages, onboarding checklist, templates, monitoring, and simple contracts/invoices.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium to High. Each client may need support, prompt tuning, workflow updates, and periodic reporting.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires sales execution and reliable delivery. Best customers are already losing time or leads to manual operations.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Very High. Customers pay precisely because the automation runs when humans are offline.
- Scalability: Medium initially; improves with templates, standard integrations, and strict scope control.

### 3. Usage-based billing for automation events

Charge based on measurable work performed: routed messages, qualified leads detected, support tickets handled, documents processed, API calls, safety checks, or monitored workflows. Usage-based billing can sit on top of a base subscription, preventing heavy users from being subsidized by light users and making infrastructure costs easier to recover.

- Initial setup cost: Medium. Needs metering, logs, customer-visible usage reports, billing thresholds, and abuse controls.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Requires accurate metering, dispute handling, monitoring, and cost-margin analysis.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Works best when customers understand the unit of value and trust the reporting.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: High. The model naturally funds continuous background work because revenue rises with workload.
- Scalability: High if unit economics are measured and pricing stays above compute/model/provider cost.

### 4. API monetization and platform integrations

Package parts of the automation stack as APIs: lead scoring, matching, moderation, routing, message summarization, alerting, or mission-control status feeds. Customers can integrate directly while ANTIGRAVITY keeps the underlying agents, queues, and fallback models operating around the clock.

- Initial setup cost: High. Requires stable API contracts, authentication, rate limits, documentation, billing, versioning, monitoring, and support.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium to High. Breaking changes, uptime expectations, and developer support become real obligations.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Needs a clearly differentiated capability; generic AI wrappers are hard to defend.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: High. APIs are inherently always-on and can fund infrastructure if pricing matches consumption.
- Scalability: High after product-market fit, but premature if built before repeatable internal use cases are proven.

### 5. Sponsorship packages and mission-control visibility

Sell sponsorship inventory around community events, reports, dashboards, newsletters, podcasts/videos, or public mission-control pages. Sponsors pay for association with a specific audience and useful visibility, not for control over the mission. Packages can include logo placement, event support, community education segments, or quarterly impact/operations reports.

- Initial setup cost: Low to Medium. Requires a sponsor deck or one-page offer, audience metrics, package tiers, and placement inventory.
- Ongoing maintenance: Low to Medium. Needs sponsor reporting, deliverable tracking, and brand-safety review.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires credible audience, community trust, and careful boundaries so sponsors do not distort product priorities.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Medium to High. Sponsorship can fund operations, but it is less automatically tied to system usage than subscriptions or metered billing.
- Scalability: Medium. Grows with audience and proof; can become meaningful but should not be the only funding leg.

### 6. Partner revenue-share / referral agreements

Create contractual partnerships with venues, community organizations, service providers, implementation partners, or complementary SaaS products. Revenue can come from partner fees, referral commissions, co-hosted events, implementation splits, or marketplace-style arrangements where ANTIGRAVITY provides automation, routing, or qualified leads.

- Initial setup cost: Medium. Requires partner targeting, agreement templates, tracking links/codes, payout logic, and clear terms.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Needs relationship management, reconciliation, performance review, and periodic renegotiation.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Works when both sides bring measurable value: audience, venues, customers, content, data, or implementation capacity.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: High if automation generates or routes the opportunities; Medium if partnerships are mostly manual events.
- Scalability: Medium to High. Strong if standardized; fragile if every deal is custom.

### 7. Non-dilutive government R&D programs

Apply for non-dilutive R&D funding such as SBIR/STTR-style programs when the work has a technical research component and public benefit angle. Relevant themes could include AI safety, child/community support infrastructure, digital trust, human verification, moderation, accessibility, resilient agent orchestration, or civic/community technology.

- Initial setup cost: High. Requires registrations, topic matching, proposal writing, budgets, compliance, reporting, and often a research plan.
- Ongoing maintenance: High during awards. Government funds usually require documentation, milestones, accounting discipline, and reporting.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Depends on company status, topic fit, agency rules, U.S. small-business requirements, and whether the work is truly R&D rather than routine product buildout.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Medium. Excellent for building core technology, but not reliable as monthly operating cash.
- Scalability: Medium. Awards can be large and non-dilutive, but cycles are slow and competitive.

### 8. Foundation, public-good, and responsible-AI grants

Pursue grants aligned with digital safety, youth/community support, AI accountability, civic technology, open-source infrastructure, privacy, or online trust. For a for-profit LLC, this is usually harder than for a nonprofit, but fiscal sponsorship, research partnerships, university collaborators, or clearly public-good deliverables can improve fit.

- Initial setup cost: Medium to High. Requires prospect research, grant narratives, budgets, outcome metrics, partner letters, and compliance review.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium to High. Reporting and restricted-use requirements can consume time.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Many funders prefer nonprofits, universities, or public-benefit entities; for-profit eligibility must be checked per opportunity.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Medium. Strong for public-good components, safety tooling, or open-source infrastructure, but weak as dependable recurring operating revenue.
- Scalability: Medium. Good grants can extend runway; they should not replace customer revenue.

### 9. Open-source sponsorships and community funding

Use GitHub Sponsors, Open Collective, Patreon-style membership, or similar channels to fund public parts of the stack: open-source agent tooling, dashboards, safety scanners, documentation, templates, or public metrics. This can also build trust with developers and community operators who benefit from the tooling.

- Initial setup cost: Low to Medium. Requires public repos, a clear sponsor profile, roadmap, recurring update cadence, and transparent use of funds.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Sponsors expect updates, issues triage, releases, and visible progress.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Works only if the public artifacts are useful enough for people or organizations to back voluntarily.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Medium. Useful as a supplemental recurring stream, especially for infrastructure and open-source maintenance.
- Scalability: Low to Medium unless the project becomes widely adopted or lands corporate sponsors.

### 10. Premium reports, data products, and derived insights

Package non-sensitive aggregate insights generated by the automated system: lead trend reports, community demand maps, SaaS request summaries, AI safety incident patterns, local event opportunity maps, or market intelligence. The product should avoid exposing private user data and should be built from consented, public, or aggregated sources.

- Initial setup cost: Medium to High. Requires data governance, aggregation, anonymization, report templates, quality checks, and sales packaging.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Needs data freshness, editorial review, privacy checks, and customer feedback loops.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires lawful data sources and a buyer who values insight enough to pay repeatedly.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: High. Always-on systems can continuously gather, clean, score, and summarize signals.
- Scalability: High if the data asset becomes differentiated; high risk if privacy or quality is weak.

### 11. Infrastructure-cost-offset compute services

Offset hardware and cloud costs by selling spare compute, hosting small workloads, batch processing, model inference, or internal tooling access when there is excess capacity. This is only attractive if it does not endanger reliability, security, or mission-critical workloads.

- Initial setup cost: Medium to High. Requires isolation, scheduling, monitoring, security hardening, billing, and capacity limits.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Hardware failures, customer workloads, security patches, and support can become operational drag.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires real spare capacity, predictable power/network costs, and strict separation from sensitive systems.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Medium. Can offset baseline costs, but should not compete with core mission workloads.
- Scalability: Low to Medium on local hardware; higher only with cloud partnerships or a deliberately built compute product.

### 12. Training, workshops, and certification packages

Sell paid workshops or cohort-based training around practical AI automation, community operations, safe agent workflows, or small-business implementation. Recordings, templates, and certification-style materials can become reusable products. Live cohorts can also feed B2B retainer leads.

- Initial setup cost: Low to Medium. Requires curriculum, landing page, checkout, scheduling, and repeatable materials.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium. Live delivery, updates, learner support, and community moderation take time.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires credible expertise and an audience willing to pay for applied guidance.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: Medium. The delivery may be human-led, but automated onboarding, reminders, grading, and support can run continuously.
- Scalability: Medium. Recorded products scale better than live workshops; live cohorts are better for trust and higher-ticket conversion.

### 13. Marketplace / success-fee model for qualified opportunities

Use automated lead discovery and matching to connect buyers with builders, automation providers, venues, or community partners. Revenue comes from listing fees, qualified-introduction fees, marketplace take rate, or success fees after closed work. This can align well with existing lead-gen tooling if quality control is strong.

- Initial setup cost: Medium. Requires lead qualification rules, matching workflows, partner onboarding, tracking, and dispute handling.
- Ongoing maintenance: Medium to High. Marketplace trust, spam prevention, and quality control are continuous responsibilities.
- Eligibility / assumptions: Requires enough supply and demand on both sides; cold-start is the main challenge.
- Fit for 24/7 automation: High. Automated discovery, scoring, routing, and follow-up are central to the model.
- Scalability: High if liquidity develops; low if manually curated forever.

## Recommended next steps

1. Create a 30-day B2B retainer pilot.
   - Define 3 packages: basic monitoring/alerting, lead-gen automation, and customer-service automation.
   - Use one fixed monthly price plus setup fee per package.
   - Target 10 warm prospects or local/community businesses and aim for 1 paying pilot.
   - Success metric: at least one customer pays recurring revenue and receives a weekly automation report.

2. Ship a simple membership/SaaS pricing test.
   - Publish 2-3 compliant tiers tied to concrete value: verification, safer matching, priority support, community/event tools, or organizer features.
   - Use Square subscription rails already planned in the architecture.
   - Success metric: 5-10 paid accounts or committed pilot users, plus churn/feedback notes.

3. Package sponsorship / partnership inventory.
   - Build a one-page offer for community partners and sponsors: event support, newsletter/report mention, dashboard visibility, or educational session support.
   - Keep mission boundaries explicit: sponsors get visibility and reporting, not governance control.
   - Success metric: 3 sponsor conversations and 1 small paid package or signed partner agreement.

4. Prepare second-wave funding assets in parallel.
   - Public open-source sponsor profile for useful automation tooling.
   - One grant-readiness folder: technical abstract, public-good outcomes, budget ranges, and partner list.
   - Data-product hypothesis list with privacy rules before any report is sold.
