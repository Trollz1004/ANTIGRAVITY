Last year I did an experiment. I downloaded every dating app I could find, paid for the cheapest paid tier on each, and tracked what I got. Twelve months, $214.30, six apps. This is what I learned and what I switched to.

## The setup

Tinder Plus. Bumble Premium. Hinge Preferred. Match. eHarmony. PlentyOfFish. Each one, the cheapest paid tier, no add-ons. I used each app for two months, then moved to the next. Same profile, same photos, same bio, same age range, same city. Tracked matches, conversations, dates, and ghosting.

## The aggregate, before anyone asks

In twelve months across six apps:

- **Matches: 481**
- **First messages sent: 412**
- **Conversations past five messages: 73**
- **Phone numbers exchanged: 19**
- **Actual dates: 6**
- **Second dates: 2**
- **Three-month relationships: 1**

That relationship came from Hinge. It ended because she moved for work. We are still friends.

## What the numbers actually say

A 15% match-to-conversation rate is normal for paid apps in 2026. An 18% conversation-to-phone-number rate is on the high end. A 31% phone-number-to-date rate is good but does not feel good when you are living it, because most of those dates came from a pool of 19 people, and a meaningful number of those 19 turned out to be either not who their photos suggested or not as serious about meeting as they suggested in their bio.

The single biggest cost was not the subscription. It was time. Counting the time spent swiping, messaging, filtering, and going on dates that did not work out, the experiment took about 80 hours. At a notional $30 an hour, that is $2,400 in time on top of the $214 in subscriptions. The real cost of a mainstream dating app in 2026 is closer to $50 an hour when you add it up.

## What was actually different across the apps

The honest answer is: not much. Tinder is the largest pool and the fastest ghost. Bumble has a slightly more serious user base and the women-message-first rule filters out the worst of the casual hookup crowd. Hinge is the most conversation-friendly app in the mainstream market and produced the only relationship I got from the experiment. Match and eHarmony are old, expensive, and full of people who joined in 2017 and have not logged in since. PlentyOfFish is what your aunt would download if she wanted to start dating again and asked a nephew for help.

The one thing all six apps had in common: a non-trivial number of profiles that were either inactive, duplicate, or clearly automated. The exact percentage varied by app, and every app's marketing denies it, but the rough shape was the same. Of the 481 matches, maybe 80 felt like real conversations with real humans. The other 400 were noise.

## What this is not

This is not a paid endorsement of youandinotai.com. I use it. The numbers above are mine. Yours will be different, and the experience will depend on your city, your age, your photos, and your bio. A small dating pool with high signal is a great fit for someone who is tired of the mainstream apps and willing to pay for verification. It is a bad fit for someone who wants maximum optionality and does not want to wait.

## What I would tell someone starting from scratch

If you are 25 to 45, you are in or near a major U.S. city, and you have been on the mainstream apps for longer than six months without a meaningful result, the answer is not "swipe more." The answer is one of two things:

1. **Delete the apps and meet people in person.** A class, a club, a sports league, a bar you actually like. The conversion rate is the same as the apps and the signal is much higher. This is what most dating coaches will tell you and they are correct.
2. **Switch to a small paid app with identity verification.** Youandinotai.com or one of the others in the same category. The pool is small. The signal is high. The cost is real money and a few minutes of signup friction. If you are serious about meeting someone, it is the best tradeoff available in 2026.

What you should not do is pay for Tinder Plus, Bumble Premium, Hinge Preferred, Match, and eHarmony all at the same time. I tried it. The math does not work.
