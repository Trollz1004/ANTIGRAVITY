Every dating app user has had this moment. You match with someone whose photos look great, their bio says something funny, and then the first message is a copy-paste opener about how you have a nice smile and they "don't usually do this but..." Or worse, they push you off the app in five messages, into Telegram or WhatsApp, where the actual scam starts.

This is a list of what to look for, in plain English, in the order you are most likely to encounter the tells. None of this is foolproof. Bots in 2026 are better than bots in 2022. But the failure modes are still recognizable if you know where to look.

## The photo tells

The most common bot photos in 2026 are not stolen from a model agency. They are generated. That means they look almost right, but not quite. Three things to check:

- **The background.** A real photo taken on a phone camera has slight distortion at the edges and lighting that matches the rest of the scene. A generated photo often has a soft, painterly background that does not match the foreground lighting. Look at the corners.
- **The teeth and eyes.** Generated faces are good at skin and bad at teeth. If the smile is wide and the teeth look slightly off, it is probably AI. Same with eyes that have a perfect symmetry no human face has.
- **Reverse image search.** Right-click, save the photo, drag it to Google Images or TinEye. If it appears on a stock site, a stock photo agency, or a totally unrelated profile with a different name, you have your answer. If TinEye returns zero results, that is also a tell — real people have their photos indexed across the web somewhere.

## The bio tells

A real bio is short, awkward, and slightly weird. A bot bio is generic, polished, and slightly weird in a different way. Compare:

- **Real:** "I coach soccer for 8-year-olds, I make a very average sourdough, and my dog thinks she runs the house. Looking for someone who laughs at the same dumb things I do."
- **Bot:** "I'm a fun-loving person who enjoys travel, good food, and great company. I work hard and play harder. Looking for someone who knows what they want in life."

The bot bio uses the words "fun-loving," "good food," and "great company" because those words match the kinds of things real humans say when they are writing a bio and do not know what to say. The real bio uses specifics that only one person could write.

## The conversation tells

This is where bots are weakest. A real person is interested in your actual answers. A bot is interested in moving you off the platform.

- **The first message is generic.** "Hey, how's your day going?" is not always a bot. But a bot will send that exact opener, then a second generic opener if you reply, then a third. A real person reads your bio or your photos and references something specific.
- **They will not answer questions.** Ask a bot what they do for a living. They will say something vague and pivot to a compliment. Ask them what they did last weekend. Same thing. Real people give weird, specific answers. Bots give round ones.
- **The pivot.** Within three to five messages, the conversation moves off the dating app. Telegram, WhatsApp, "let me text you," "here's my number." The reason is always the same: the platform is harder to spam, the off-platform channel is easier to monetize. Some real people do this too, but the speed of the pivot is a tell. A real person will chat on the dating app for a week or more before suggesting a switch.
- **The "are you real" test.** Ask them to take a selfie holding up three fingers. A real person will do it, badly, in about 30 seconds. A bot will not reply or will deflect.

## The profile behavior tells

- **They message you first, immediately.** Right after a match. No real person is sitting at their phone refreshing a dating app at 3 a.m. Bots are.
- **They never suggest a specific place to meet.** They will say "we should hang out sometime." Real people, after enough conversation, will say "there's a coffee shop on 5th, want to go Saturday at 2?"
- **Their profile is too complete.** Six photos, every prompt answered, every section filled in. Real people leave sections blank.

## What you can do about it

The first defense is you, with the checklist above. The second defense is the app you are using. A dating app that does identity verification at signup — a real ID, a live selfie check, paid verification — is hostile to bots by design, because the bots cannot pass it. The third defense is to trust your gut. If the conversation feels like a script, it is a script. Real people are weird. Real people are slow. Real people do not always have a perfect opening line. If the profile is gorgeous, the opener is smooth, the conversation moves fast, and the pivot comes in five messages, walk away. Your time is more valuable than a stranger's algorithm.
