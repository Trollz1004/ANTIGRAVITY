# How to move from chatting to a first meeting

Moving from chatting in a dating app to a first meeting in person helps you find out if a match is genuine. People searching for how to move from chatting to a first meeting encounter profiles that chat for weeks but never show up. Scammers and automated accounts use scripts and stolen photos to keep conversations going without ever agreeing to a face-to-face date. On February 13, 2024, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/02/love-stinks-when-scammer-involved) wrote that scammers tell typical lies about why they cannot meet you. The Federal Trade Commission advises people never to send money, cryptocurrency, gift cards, or wire transfers to anyone they have not met in person. Planning an early in-person meeting helps verify that your match is a real person living in your area.

## Signs a match is ready to meet in person

A genuine match is comfortable taking the conversation into the real world after establishing basic rapport.

1. The person answers specific questions about their local area and daily routine.
2. The conversation shows mutual curiosity about hobbies, work, and shared interests.
3. The match agrees to a short conversation by voice or video before meeting.
4. The person responds favorably when you suggest a local public venue.
5. The match does not ask you for money, gift cards, or financial assistance.

## Warning signs from fake profiles and scammers

Fake profiles and scam operators follow patterns designed to avoid in-person contact while building unearned trust.

1. **Excuses to avoid meeting:** Scammers claim to be traveling abroad, working on offshore projects, or dealing with sudden personal emergencies when you propose a date.
2. **Pressure to leave the platform:** A scammer quickly asks to move to an off-platform messaging application or encrypted text thread before you meet.
3. **Requests for money:** The contact invents a crisis, medical bill, or travel expense and asks you to send funds before an arranged meeting.
4. **Refusal of live video:** The person refuses a brief video check or claims their phone camera is broken.
5. **Inconsistent background details:** Stories about their home city, schedule, or workplace do not match earlier statements.

## Steps to plan a first in-person meeting

Setting up a simple meeting protects your schedule and gives both people a relaxed environment.

1. Suggest a short daytime meetup for coffee, tea, or a walk in a public park.
2. Select a busy public venue with easy parking or transit access.
3. Set a clear timeframe of thirty to forty-five minutes so expectations stay low.
4. Arrange your own transportation to and from the meeting location.
5. Tell a friend or family member the date, time, and location of your meeting.
