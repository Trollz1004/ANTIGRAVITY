# How to set a first date budget you are comfortable with

Meeting someone new should not create financial strain. A first date is a chance to see if two people share mutual respect and basic conversational chemistry. High-pressure settings with expensive menus can create awkward expectations before you know each other. On February 13, 2024, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/02/love-stinks-when-scammer-involved) published an article titled "Love Stinks – when a scammer is involved". The article stated that romance fraud caused $1.14 billion in reported losses in 2023 across 64,003 reports, with a median loss of $2,000 per person. Establishing a personal spending boundary keeps your first meeting simple and protects your wallet.

## Why a simple first date budget works

Spending less money on a first meeting keeps the focus on genuine conversation.

1. Reduces financial pressure: Low-cost activities remove anxiety about who pays a large check.
2. Keeps meetings brief: A casual drink or walk provides a natural endpoint after forty-five minutes.
3. Lowers risk: Keeping early dates inexpensive prevents financial regret if a match is not a fit.
4. Identifies shared values: A person who respects your spending limits demonstrates courtesy and practical judgment.
5. Avoids fraud traps: Low-cost public dates deter actors who search for extravagant spending.

## How to choose a budget that fits your comfort level

Pick a dollar target before you propose an activity.

1. Set a personal limit: Decide the maximum amount you want to spend before suggesting a plan.
2. Select daytime counter service: Coffee shops and bakeries allow each person to order and pay at the counter.
3. Propose free public spaces: Public parks, art walks, and botanical gardens cost zero dollars.
4. Avoid multi-course dinners: Full dinners lock both people into long meals with expensive bills.
5. Plan your own transportation: Pay for your own ride or parking so you can leave at any point.

## How to suggest affordable plans without discomfort

Clear communication makes low-cost plans straightforward.

1. Propose a specific casual spot: Suggest a neighborhood cafe rather than an open-ended dinner.
2. State the plan plainly: Mention grabbing tea or looking at an outdoor gallery.
3. Plan to pay your own share: Arrive prepared to cover your own beverage or entry fee.
4. Decline upscale upgrades politely: If a match suggests a luxury restaurant, redirect to a simpler venue.
5. Watch for entitled reactions: If a match demands an expensive venue, end the conversation.

## What to do if money issues arise

Boundaries keep dating safe and enjoyable.

1. Pay for yourself: Covering your own tab prevents unwanted obligations.
2. Never lend money: Decline requests to cover a match's emergency, transportation, or bills.
3. Never discuss private assets: Do not share your bank balances, home value, or salary details.
4. Leave if you feel uncomfortable: You can step away if a person acts pushy about spending.
5. Block and report bad actors: Use reporting tools if an account tries to extract funds.
