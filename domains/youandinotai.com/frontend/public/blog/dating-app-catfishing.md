# Dating app catfishing: the signs and the one-dollar fix

Dealing with dating app catfishing wastes more than a single evening. A catfish operator builds a fabricated persona using someone else's photos, mirrors your interests to create fast rapport, and stretches out text conversations for weeks while avoiding every opportunity to meet in person.

## Five warning signs of a catfish profile

Most catfishing accounts give themselves away during the first few days of conversation if you watch for inconsistencies between their claims and their actions.

1. The photo set contains only two or three polished portraits with no casual snapshots from local parks, restaurants, or hobbies.
2. The match claims to live in your city yet cannot name a single cross street, coffee shop, or weekend market nearby.
3. Every plan for a thirty-minute coffee meetup falls apart at the last hour due to a sudden work trip, family crisis, or broken car.
4. Their stories shift slightly from day to day regarding their job title, how long they have lived in town, or their daily routine.
5. They press for your personal phone number or external chat handle before agreeing to a simple daytime meeting.

When a match cancels two straightforward coffee plans in a row without proposing a concrete makeup day, stop investing emotional energy in the thread.
