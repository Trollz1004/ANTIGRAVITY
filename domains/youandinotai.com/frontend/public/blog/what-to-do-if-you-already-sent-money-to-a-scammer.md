# What to do if you already sent money to a scammer on a dating app

Realizing that you sent funds to a scammer on a dating app causes immediate distress. Fraud operators create profiles with stolen pictures and scripts to build trust and ask for emergency financial assistance. In August 2022, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams) published consumer advice explaining what steps to take if you paid someone who scammed you on a dating app or social media site. The Federal Trade Commission advises people who sent funds to contact the company used to send the payment immediately, report the transaction as fraud, and ask if the transaction can be reversed. Taking quick steps helps you protect your remaining funds and secure your personal accounts.

## Steps to take immediately after sending money

Acting quickly gives you the best chance to halt pending transfers or document the theft.

1. Stop all communication with the profile right away.
2. Contact the bank, credit card company, or wire service used to send the money.
3. Tell the financial company the payment was fraud and ask for a reversal.
4. Contact the gift card issuer or payment app company if you used those payment methods.
5. Save records of transaction receipts, account names, and transfer confirmation numbers.

## How to protect your accounts and identity

Scammers sometimes seek further access to your financial accounts or personal credentials after an initial payment.

1. Change passwords for email, banking, and dating profiles.
2. Check your bank and credit card accounts for unauthorized transactions.
3. File a report with the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
4. Submit a fraud report to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov.
5. Notify local police to create an official crime report.

## Warning signs of follow-up recovery scams

People who lose money to fraud can become targets for secondary schemes known as recovery scams.

1. Fraud operators reach out claiming they can recover your lost money for an advance fee.
2. The contact claims to work with law enforcement or private recovery agencies.
3. The person demands payment in cryptocurrency, gift cards, or wire transfers to begin work.
4. Refuse any unsolicited offers to recover lost funds.
