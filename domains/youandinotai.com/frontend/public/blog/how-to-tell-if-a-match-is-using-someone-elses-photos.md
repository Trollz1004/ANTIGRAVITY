# How to tell if a match is using someone else's photos

Dating profiles sometimes feature pictures that belong to someone else. Dishonest accounts take images from social media profiles, commercial photo websites, or modeling pages. In guidance dated August 2022, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams) stated that romance scammers create fake profiles on dating sites and apps. The Federal Trade Commission advises running a reverse image search on a profile picture to see if the photo connects to another name or details that do not match what the person told you. Learning how to check profile pictures helps you identify accounts that rely on stolen images.

## Inspect the photos for visual inconsistencies

Accounts that borrow images from other sources display noticeable visual signs.

1. The profile shows pictures with different ages, facial structures, or hair colors across the set.
2. The image collection includes only studio headshots or magazine pictures with professional studio lighting.
3. Every photo shows low resolution, blurry edges, or visible crop lines from a screenshot.
4. The background locations display distant landmarks that do not match the city listed on the profile.
5. The account holder shares no casual pictures taken with friends, family, or local surroundings.

Genuine people share casual snapshots that match their age and daily environment.

## Ask for a live verification or a video call

Profiles that use another person's pictures avoid real-time camera interactions.

1. Propose a short video call before planning an in-person meeting.
2. Suggest exchanging a live photo where each person holds up a specific number of fingers.
3. Accounts using stolen pictures offer excuses about broken cameras, low bandwidth, or strict workplace policies.
4. The match promises to call later but cancels each video conversation at the last minute.
5. A refusal to appear on camera indicates that the person does not look like the photos on the profile.

Real matches welcome a quick video greeting to confirm each person is real.

## Watch for sudden financial requests or off-platform moves

Impostor accounts use copied pictures to build trust and ask for money.

1. The person asks to move conversation to an external messaging service right after matching.
2. The sender claims sudden travel troubles, hospital bills, or customs fees and asks for emergency funds.
3. The match requests payment through wire transfers, gift cards, or cryptocurrency.
4. Stop messaging any account that asks for money or personal financial details.
5. Block and report the profile through the app interface when photos do not belong to the sender.
