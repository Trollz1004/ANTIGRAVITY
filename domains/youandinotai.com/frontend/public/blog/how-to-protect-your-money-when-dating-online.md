# How to protect your money when dating online

Online dating connects people who want genuine relationships. Protecting your finances requires clear personal rules. In August 2022, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams) published consumer guidance titled "What to Know About Romance Scams". The Federal Trade Commission stated that romance scammers create fake profiles, strike up relationships to build trust, make up reasons they cannot meet in person, and ask for money. On February 13, 2024, the Federal Trade Commission published an article titled ["Love Stinks – when a scammer is involved"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/02/love-stinks-when-scammer-involved). The article stated that romance fraud caused $1.14 billion in reported losses in 2023 across 64,003 reports, with a median loss of $2,000 per person. Learning how to protect your funds keeps you in control.

## Rules to protect your money

Simple rules prevent financial loss when you date online.

1. Never send money to an online match: Refuse requests for cash, bank transfers, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.
2. Keep financial accounts private: Never share your bank account details, credit card numbers, or investment logins.
3. Decline requests to buy gift cards: Scammers ask for retail gift cards because gift cards function like cash.
4. Refuse investment advice from matches: Fraud operators invent fake cryptocurrency platforms and fake investment schemes.
5. Do not cash checks for someone else: Depositing a check from an online match can leave you responsible for stolen funds.

## Warning signs of financial requests

Watch for specific behaviors that lead up to a request for funds.

1. Declarations of love before meeting: The person rushes emotional intimacy to gain leverage.
2. Inability to meet in person: The contact claims to live abroad, work on an oil rig, or serve overseas.
3. Sudden emergencies: The match asks for funds to pay for hospital bills, plane tickets, or customs fees.
4. Pressure to leave the dating app: The person asks to switch to personal email, phone text, or external chat platforms.
5. Requests for account access: The individual asks to borrow an account or transfer money through your bank.

## What to do if a match asks for money

Take immediate steps if a match asks for money or financial support.

1. Say no immediately: Refuse the request and do not negotiate or explain.
2. Stop all communication: Cut contact across every channel you shared.
3. Use platform reporting tools: Block and report the account so moderators can review the profile.
4. Talk to someone you trust: Share the situation with a friend or family member before acting.
5. Contact law enforcement if funds left your account: Report theft to your bank, local police, and the Federal Trade Commission.
