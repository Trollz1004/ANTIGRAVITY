# How to avoid dating app wire transfer scams and emergency money requests

Dating app wire transfer scams target singles looking for honest connections online. Fraud operators build quick online rapport and then invent sudden personal emergencies to request urgent bank wires. On February 9, 2023, a [Federal Trade Commission Data Spotlight](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2023/02/romance-scammers-favorite-lies-exposed) reported that the most common lie told by romance scammers was claiming that they or someone close to them was hurt, sick, or in jail. The publication explained that fraudsters use invented medical or legal emergencies to ask matches for money. Understanding how these requests unfold helps you protect your funds.

## Why scammers demand wire transfers

Fraud operators prefer wire transfers because wire payments move rapidly and cannot be canceled once claimed.

1. Bank wires transfer money directly into the recipient account without delay.
2. Wire transfers cannot be reversed once processed by the receiving bank.
3. Scammers use overseas accounts or intermediary money mules to obscure the final destination.
4. The payment requires no credit card authorization or merchant dispute process.
5. Operators can withdraw cash immediately at a local branch or transfer it elsewhere.

Once an international or domestic wire leaves your bank account, recovering the money is nearly impossible.

## Warning signs of emergency money requests

Scammers create high emotional pressure so you send funds before verifying the situation. Watch for these patterns in your messages.

1. The match professes deep affection quickly but claims to work abroad, on an offshore site, or in a remote area.
2. The person encounters a sudden medical emergency, customs delay, or legal complication requiring instant cash.
3. The match asks you to send a wire transfer through Western Union, MoneyGram, or direct bank transfer.
4. The person insists that they will repay you as soon as an inheritance or paycheck clears.
5. The match becomes angry or emotionally manipulative if you hesitate or suggest alternative assistance.

A legitimate date will never ask an online match to wire emergency money.

## Steps to take when a match asks for money

Setting clear boundaries keeps your finances safe when meeting new people online.

1. Refuse every request to wire funds, transfer money, or provide account numbers.
2. Stop messaging any profile that introduces financial distress into the conversation.
3. Never share your bank routing numbers or personal identity details with online matches.
4. Use built-in platform options to block and report suspicious accounts right away.
5. File a formal complaint with consumer protection agencies and banking fraud divisions if you sent funds.
