# How to avoid dating app cryptocurrency scams and fake investment schemes

Dating app cryptocurrency scams target singles looking for honest connections. Fraud operators create fake profiles to gain trust and steer conversations toward financial speculation. In February 2024, the [Federal Trade Commission report](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/02/love-stinks-when-scammer-involved) stated that romance fraud reports showed scammers steering people into bogus cryptocurrency investment schemes. Recognizing these patterns helps people spot fraud early.

## How crypto romance fraud unfolds

The fraud follows a regular pattern designed to lower your guard before introducing money.

1. A match expresses strong romantic interest early in the exchange.
2. The person claims great success trading digital assets or cryptocurrency.
3. The match offers to show you how to trade on an unfamiliar platform or mobile application.
4. The website displays fabricated profits to encourage larger deposits.
5. Account withdrawals are blocked unless you pay extra fees or taxes.

Once funds move to an unregulated wallet, the operator stops responding.

## Common warning signs in messages

Scammers use predictable scripts when trying to move money. Watch for these signals in early messages.

1. The match pivots from personal hobbies to investment advice within days.
2. The person claims guaranteed profits with zero financial risk.
3. The match urges you to transfer funds into a private digital wallet.
4. The person refuses to meet in person or join a live video conversation.
5. The match insists that secrecy protects your shared financial future.

## Steps to take when a match mentions investments

Clear boundaries protect you from financial fraud online.

1. Refuse every request to invest money, purchase cryptocurrency, or send funds.
2. Stop messaging any profile that offers unsolicited financial coaching.
3. Do not enter personal banking details into websites sent by a match.
4. Block and report suspicious profiles using platform tools.
5. Report fraud attempts to consumer protection authorities and local law enforcement.
