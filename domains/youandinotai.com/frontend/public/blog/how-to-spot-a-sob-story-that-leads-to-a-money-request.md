# How to spot a sob story that leads to a money request

Connecting with someone on a dating app should center on mutual interests and honest conversation. When an online match shares an intense personal crisis early, pause and evaluate the conversation. On February 9, 2023, the Federal Trade Commission published a Data Spotlight titled ["Romance scammers' favorite lies exposed"](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2023/02/romance-scammers-favorite-lies-exposed). The report stated that nearly 70,000 consumers reported romance scams in 2022, with reported losses reaching $1.3 billion. The publication stated that 24 percent of people who reported a romance scam said the scammer told them that they or someone close to them was sick, hurt, or in jail. On February 13, 2024, the Federal Trade Commission published an article titled ["Love Stinks – when a scammer is involved"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/02/love-stinks-when-scammer-involved). The article stated that romance fraud caused $1.14 billion in reported losses in 2023 across 64,003 reports, with a median loss of $2,000 per person. Recognizing fabricated personal crises helps keep your finances secure.

## Fabricated crisis scripts used to ask for money

Scammers use predictable narratives to evoke sympathy before asking for money.

1. Medical emergencies: The match claims a sudden surgery, hospital bill, or prescription cost requires outside financial help.
2. Legal troubles: The individual claims to be detained, jailed, or stuck in customs and needs bail or release fees.
3. Sudden travel or work disasters: The person claims an offshore work contract, stranded vehicle, or lost travel ticket prevents movement.
4. Frozen bank accounts: The contact claims local banks blocked their funds and asks for temporary funds until accounts reopen.
5. Family hardships: The match describes a sick child or an injured relative who lacks basic medical care.

## Warning signs that a personal story leads to a financial ask

Look for specific conversation habits that accompany a manufactured crisis.

1. Rapid emotional bonding: The match professes deep feelings or soulmate connections within days.
2. Constant excuses to avoid meeting: The person claims to work overseas or on an offshore project to explain why in-person meetings cannot happen.
3. Sudden urgency: The crisis demands immediate payment within hours to prevent disaster.
4. Requests for irreversible payment methods: The person asks for funds through wire transfers, gift cards, or cryptocurrency.
5. Isolation attempts: The contact asks you to keep the situation confidential from friends and family.

## Clear steps to take when a match shares a crisis

Maintain clear personal boundaries when a conversation turns toward financial hardship.

1. State your personal rule: Say that you do not send money to people you have not met in person.
2. Refuse every payment request: Decline requests for cash, gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.
3. Do not offer loans or alternatives: Refuse to deposit checks or make payments on behalf of the match.
4. Stop communicating: Cease contact if the person responds with anger, guilt, or persistence.
5. Use platform tools: Block the profile and report the account so administrators can review the activity.
