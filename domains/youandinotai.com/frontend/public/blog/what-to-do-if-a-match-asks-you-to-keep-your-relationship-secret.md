# What to do if a match asks you to keep your relationship secret

A match asking you to keep your relationship secret is a serious warning sign in online dating. In August 2022, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams) published consumer guidance on romance scams advising people to talk to someone they trust and ask friends or family if they are concerned about a new love interest. Scammers push for secrecy to isolate people from outside advice before asking for money. Recognizing requests for secrecy protects your personal well-being.

## Why scammers ask for secrecy

Scammers demand secrecy to control conversations and prevent outside intervention.

1. Secrecy stops trusted friends and family from reviewing conversations or profile claims.
2. An isolated contact cannot easily check suspicious stories with outside observers.
3. Secret communication makes it easier for an operator to request money without interruption.
4. Keeping the connection quiet prevents others from suggesting a reverse image search.
5. Scammers use false emotional urgency to make secrecy feel like personal loyalty.

## What to do if a match asks for secrecy

Take immediate steps if an online match asks you to hide the relationship from friends or family.

1. Talk to a trusted friend, family member, or counselor about the match.
2. Refuse requests to hide conversations or keep interactions private from loved ones.
3. Observe how the person reacts when you decline to keep secrets.
4. Stop communicating if the match pressures you, becomes angry, or demands secrecy.
5. Use platform features to block and report accounts that demand hidden relationships.

## Warning signs connected to secret relationships

Pay attention to behavioral patterns that accompany requests for secrecy.

1. The match professes intense affection or love within days of connecting.
2. The person provides excuses to avoid meeting in person or appearing on live video.
3. The match claims to work overseas, on an oil rig, or in the military.
4. The individual invents a financial emergency or legal crisis.
5. The contact asks for gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency payments.
