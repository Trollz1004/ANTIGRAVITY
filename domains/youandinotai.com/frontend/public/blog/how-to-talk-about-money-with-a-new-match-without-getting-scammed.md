# How to talk about money with a new match without getting scammed

Talking about money with a new match can feel uncomfortable. Healthy dating conversations sometimes touch on careers, lifestyle habits, and plans for a first date. Financial predators use those same conversations to probe your finances and set up scams. On February 13, 2024, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/02/love-stinks-when-scammer-involved) published an article titled "Love Stinks – when a scammer is involved". The article stated that romance fraud caused $1.14 billion in reported losses in 2023 across 64,003 reports, with a median loss of $2,000 per person. Clear personal rules keep your money safe while you get to know someone new.

## Healthy money conversations versus fraud probing

Normal matches respect your privacy. Fraud operators ask targeted questions to measure your wealth.

1. General career talk is normal: Sharing what field you work in is typical conversation.
2. Specific income questions are warning signs: A new match should not ask for your exact salary or your bank account balances.
3. Lifestyle preferences are safe to discuss: Talking about favorite affordable restaurants or free public parks helps plan dates.
4. Asset discussions are red flags: Questions about home equity, retirement accounts, or stock holdings indicate financial probing.
5. Splitting the bill is standard: Agreeing to pay for your own coffee or meal keeps expectations equal.

## Rules for discussing finances with a new match

Set firm limits before you start chatting.

1. Keep account numbers private: Never disclose bank details, credit card numbers, or online payment handles.
2. Never send money to an online match: Refuse every request for cash, gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.
3. Decline investment pitches: Do not take financial guidance, crypto advice, or trading tips from someone you met online.
4. Keep early dates simple: Plan a low-cost first meeting in a public space to avoid financial pressure.
5. Do not share financial paperwork: Never send pay stubs, tax forms, or credit reports to an online contact.

## Warning signs when money enters the conversation

Watch for specific patterns that reveal bad intentions.

1. Sudden personal emergencies: The match asks for funds to cover a hospital bill, travel ticket, or car repair.
2. Requests for financial favors: The person asks you to deposit a check, forward a transfer, or accept packages.
3. Claims of great trading wealth: The contact boasts about secret investment systems and promises guaranteed returns.
4. Excuses that prevent meeting in person: The individual claims work on an overseas oil rig or military post to explain why in-person meetings cannot happen.
5. Emotional anger when you refuse: The match acts offended or accuses you of lacking trust when you decline to send money.

## What to do if a match asks for money

Take immediate action if an exchange turns into a request for funds.

1. Say no clearly: State that you do not send money to online contacts.
2. End the conversation: Stop sending replies and do not explain your decision further.
3. Block and report the account: Use platform tools so moderators can review the profile.
4. Inform your financial institution: If you shared banking details, notify your bank immediately to secure your accounts.
5. File a report with consumer agencies: Submit a complaint through government fraud reporting channels.
