Where to Recycle NAS Units and External Drives in Central Florida

Upgrading shared network storage or retiring desktop backup enclosures leaves surplus drive arrays behind. Businesses and home offices across Central Florida can recycle network attached storage boxes and external media with verified data handling.

Step 1: Identifying Storage Enclosures and Drives

Accepted NAS Hardware and External Storage Arrays

Network attached storage boxes and desktop external arrays serve critical file-sharing and backup roles, but eventually get replaced by larger arrays or modern server hardware. OnlineRecycle.net handles both complete storage systems and loose drives.

We accept desktop NAS units, multi-bay enclosure towers, external arrays, and rackmount storage shelves. Along with full enclosures, we accept internal parts including server motherboards, processors, RAM modules, and power cables.

When preparing storage enclosures for handover, keep drive caddies, power supplies, and connection cables grouped with each unit. Leaving drives installed inside their bays or placing loose drives in a sturdy box ensures smooth handling during intake.

Local design studios, legal offices, and professional practices in Central Florida routinely upgrade shared file servers to keep up with growing storage needs. When a multi-bay desktop array or rack storage shelf reaches end-of-life, the hardware still contains metal enclosures, internal backplanes, and power components that can be processed responsibly.

Step 2: Media Separation and Processing

Storage Wiped or Destroyed at Intake

Because NAS enclosures hold multi-terabyte collections of company databases, customer files, tax documentation, and system image backups, proper storage media handling is mandatory.

OnlineRecycle.net processes all common storage form factors. We accept 3.5-inch and 2.5-inch SATA hard disk drives, enterprise SAS drives, SATA solid-state drives, and NVMe M.2 modules. At intake, all data-bearing drives are removed and isolated from enclosure caddies and chassis.

We make reasonable efforts to wipe or destroy data using standard processes. Media undergoes multi-pass erasure or physical destruction before any equipment undergoes chassis refurbishment. Organizations that routinely decommission storage arrays can depend on systematic drive separation for all accepted units.

Unlike basic consumer electronics, network attached storage arrays combine multi-drive sets that span several physical disks. Every individual drive bay, SATA connector, and NVMe slot is checked during intake. Solid-state drives and magnetic platters are removed from caddies and separated from chassis components before erasure or destruction begins.
