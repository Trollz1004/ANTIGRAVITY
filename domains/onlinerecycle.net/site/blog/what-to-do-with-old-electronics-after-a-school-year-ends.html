What to Do with Old Electronics After a School Year Ends

When a school year ends, households across Central Florida often find old school laptops, desktop towers, tablets, and chargers taking up desk space. Planning an electronics cleanout clears room for the summer.

Step 1: Inventory and Sort

Gather and Group Student and Household Devices

Students often finish classes with extra electronics left over from school projects and homework stations. Desks, backpacks, and closets tend to hold unused devices once the semester closes. Operated by Trash Or Treasure Online Recycler LLC in Sorrento, Florida, OnlineRecycle.net provides free drop-off in Sorrento and free scheduled pickup across Lake County, Orange County, and Seminole County.

Start by collecting all computers in one spot. Group desktop towers, workstations, all-in-ones, MacBooks, and notebooks. Look through drawers for loose components such as motherboards, GPUs, RAM, and processors. If a student maintained a home lab or media server, collect rackmount servers, NAS units, enterprise switches, routers, and firewalls.

Next, gather student tablets, phones, and study peripherals. Gather smartphones, tablets, computer monitors, docking stations, printers, cables, and UPS battery backups. Keeping cords and power supplies with their matching units keeps your intake batch organized.

Then, collect entertainment gear and media. Gather gaming consoles, handheld game systems, controllers, and gaming accessories used during the school term. Gather video game discs, cartridges, DVDs, Blu-rays, 4K UHD discs, and audio CDs. For audio setups, collect stereo receivers, power amplifiers, bookshelf speakers, floor speakers, soundbars, subwoofers, car stereos, and head units.

Step 2: Storage Media and Data Selection

Identify Storage Drives and Select Data Handling Preferences

Laptops, desktop PCs, and family computers used during the school year often store classwork, school accounts, personal photos, and family records. Save any files you need onto your own personal backup drive before scheduling intake.

Check which equipment contains digital storage media. Devices may contain NVMe M.2 modules, SATA SSD drives, 2.5-inch or 3.5-inch hard drives, or SAS enterprise drives. At OnlineRecycle.net, solid-state drives and magnetic hard drives are separated at intake. Storage media is wiped or destroyed with reasonable effort before any equipment refurbishment or material recovery proceeds.

When completing the booking form, select the data handling preference that matches your equipment:

  • Standard intake & storage wipe/destruction: For general batches containing student computers or mixed storage drives.
  • Includes laptops/PCs/drives needing priority data wipe: For machines holding sensitive personal files or student records.
  • No storage media (monitors, printers, cables, peripherals): For batches containing only screens, audio hardware, printers, or cords.
Step 3: Staging and Scheduling

Count Your Items and Choose Drop-Off or Route Pickup

Count your staged equipment to choose the matching volume tier on the scheduling form. OnlineRecycle.net organizes intake into four batch tiers: 1-5 items for small batches, 6-15 items for study area cleanouts, 16-50 items for larger household cleanouts, and 50+ items for commercial pallet loads.

Intake hours run seven days a week, Monday through Sunday, from 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM. Choose the intake option that fits your schedule:

  • Sorrento Drop-Off: Nine daily 30-minute intake windows between 8:00 AM and 5:30 PM.
  • Central FL Route Pickup: Five daily 2-hour windows across Lake County, Orange County, and Seminole County.

Submitting the booking form confirms your slot. The system generates a booking reference ID and a downloadable .ICS calendar file with an Add to Google Calendar link.

After intake, tested working hardware is refurbished for resale. Non-working boards, chassis steel, aluminum, and copper wiring enter commodity material recovery.
