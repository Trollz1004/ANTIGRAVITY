How to Wipe a Hard Drive Before Recycling a PC

Before you hand over a desktop tower, laptop, or workstation for recycling, taking a few careful steps to back up files and wipe your drive protects your personal and business information.

Step 1: Back Up and Sign Out

Save Your Files Before Erasing the Drive

Deleting desktop shortcuts or emptying the recycle bin does not remove data from a hard drive or solid-state drive. Before you wipe a PC, copy documents, photos, accounting records, and browser bookmarks to an external backup drive or your replacement computer. Verify that you can open those backed-up files on another machine before erasing anything.

Once your files are backed up, sign out of mail clients, cloud storage folders, and licensed software accounts. Deauthorizing the old PC prevents account lockouts after the hardware leaves your home or office. Remember to check optical drives for any forgotten CDs, DVDs, Blu-rays, or game discs and remove any flash drives or memory cards plugged into the ports.

Step 2: Identify Every Storage Device

Check for Both Solid-State and Magnetic Drives

Modern desktops and workstations often hold more than one storage device. A tower or notebook may boot from an NVMe M.2 or SATA SSD while storing larger folders on a secondary 2.5-inch or 3.5-inch magnetic hard drive. Office servers and NAS units may also contain multiple SAS enterprise drives or external arrays.

Open your operating system disk management view or BIOS hardware list to see every connected drive inside the chassis. When you run a factory reset or drive erasure tool, make sure you select and clear each installed drive rather than only the primary boot volume.

Step 3: Run a Full Reset

Use Built-In Full Drive Erasure Options

On a working Windows PC, use the built-in reset feature and choose the option to remove everything and fully clean the drive rather than just removing your files. The full clean option overwrites storage sectors across the drive instead of simply clearing the file table. On a MacBook or desktop Mac, sign out of your accounts and run a factory reset, using Disk Utility secure overwrite options on magnetic hard drives where available rather than a quick format alone.

What if the PC no longer turns on, has a broken motherboard, or fails to boot into the operating system? You do not need to buy special adapters or throw the computer into the trash. You can remove the drive if you prefer, or keep it inside the case and flag the batch for priority data handling when scheduling intake with OnlineRecycle.net.

Step 4: Intake Separation and Destruction
