Welcome to Yesterday's News Today for August 10, 2026. Five stories you might have missed — all five reported by Good News Network.

First, in space: NASA engineers found a way to free up power aboard Voyager 2, shutting down select components and switching to lower-energy options while keeping the spacecraft warm enough to function. The fix buys the nearly fifty-year-old probe another year of scientific work.

In wildlife research, a team from the Elephant Reintegration Trust of South Africa reports that zoo elephants returned to the wild develop successful coping mechanisms and can readapt to wildlife systems — even after long-term captivity.

In health, researchers at Binghamton University in New York are studying the drug Vamorolone in very young boys with Duchenne muscular dystrophy. Early findings show encouraging motor improvements in just twelve weeks, suggesting treatment before obvious symptoms appear could make a significant difference.

At the Toronto Zoo, an endangered pygmy hippopotamus was born after about an hour and a half of active labor — and the calf was teetering toward its feet within fifteen minutes.

And one from the history file: 180 years ago on August 10, the Smithsonian Institution was first chartered, named for British scientist James Smithson, whose bequest created the Washington, D.C. museum complex.
